RESEARCH QUESTION
-----------------
How vulnerable are the six GCC member states to Water-Energy-Food 
(WEF) Nexus shocks, and which countries face the most critical 
systemic risks as of 2023?

METHODOLOGY
-----------
1. Data sourced from World Bank World Development Indicators (2024)
   covering 6 indicators across 6 GCC countries from 2000 to 2023.
2. Indicators normalised to a 0-10 vulnerability scale using 
   Min-Max normalisation.
3. Composite WEF Index computed as weighted average:
   Water (35%) + Energy (35%) + Food (30%).
4. Country averages computed over 2018-2023 for stability.

KEY FINDINGS
------------
Rank 1 - Saudi Arabia  (WEF Index: 6.38) — Energy transition gap
Rank 2 - Bahrain       (WEF Index: 6.02) — Extreme water scarcity
Rank 3 - Kuwait        (WEF Index: 5.34) — Balanced vulnerability
Rank 4 - Oman          (WEF Index: 4.13) — Food import dependency
Rank 5 - Qatar         (WEF Index: 4.08) — Moderate across all
Rank 6 - UAE           (WEF Index: 2.39) — Least vulnerable

HEADLINE INSIGHTS
-----------------
1. Saudi Arabia's biggest vulnerability is NOT water — it is the 
   widest gap between renewable energy ambition and actual delivery
   in the entire GCC (energy score: 9.95/10).

2. Bahrain faces the most acute water crisis of any GCC member 
   (water score: 9.96/10), compounded by its status as a low-lying
   island exposed to sea-level rise.

3. UAE's early investment in nuclear (Barakah) and solar energy 
   is already measurably reducing its systemic vulnerability —
   the only GCC member with an energy score below 1.0.

4. Kuwait is the only GCC country with effectively zero renewable
   electricity output — confirmed by a renewable score of 0.00,
   making its energy transition the most urgent in the region.

5. Oman's relatively low overall vulnerability masks a critical 
   food security weakness (food score: 9.23) driven by high 
   import dependency and limited agricultural capacity.

POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS
----------------------
Saudi Arabia: Accelerate renewable deployment beyond Vision 2030 
targets — current trajectory is insufficient given energy score.

Bahrain: Prioritise solar-powered desalination to break the 
fossil fuel-water dependency spiral. Explore regional water 
cooperation framework with GCC neighbours.

Kuwait: Implement the 1.1 GW Shagaya solar project immediately —
zero renewable output is fiscally and environmentally unsustainable.

UAE: Share Barakah and solar financing model with GCC peers —
demonstrated success warrants regional knowledge transfer.

Oman: Leverage green hydrogen revenues to fund food security 
buffers and strategic food reserves.

Qatar: LNG expansion revenues should be ring-fenced partially 
for renewable transition — the only GCC member with no net-zero 
target represents a growing reputational and fiscal risk.
